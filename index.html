What the model assumes

  • The floor has 2 resuscitation bays, 9 acute care beds and 4 fast track beds around a central nurses' station, plus one triage room, one CT scanner and a 15-seat waiting room.
  • Arrivals follow a typical ED daily curve, quiet before dawn and busiest from late morning into the evening. About 15% come by ambulance and skip the waiting room.
  • Acuity uses the five-level Emergency Severity Index. ESI 1 goes to resuscitation, ESI 2 and 3 to acute care, ESI 4 and 5 to fast track, with overflow between zones when one is full.
  • Each step needs the right person at the bedside: a nurse for the first assessment, labs and medications, and discharge teaching; a provider for the evaluation and the disposition. Patients wait in bed when that person is busy.
  • Some patients go to CT during their workup. Admitted patients board in their ED bed until an inpatient bed opens, and one cleaning tech turns over each bed before the next patient.
  • Providers drop to half strength from 23:00 to 07:00. Lower-acuity patients leave without being seen when the wait outlasts their patience.
  • Walking is sped up for visibility and does not add to visit times. All patients and timings are generated, and the parameters are illustrative, not calibrated to a real hospital.
